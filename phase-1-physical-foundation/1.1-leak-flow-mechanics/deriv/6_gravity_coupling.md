# 6. Gravity Coupling from Information Flow

## Information Potential

Define an information potential proportional to information density:

\[
\Phi_I = \lambda s
\]

---

## Force from Potential Gradient

Force density arises from gradients:

\[
F_I = -\nabla \Phi_I
\]

Substituting:

\[
F_I = -\lambda \nabla s
\]

Information gradients therefore generate forces.

---

## Flux Definition

Information flux is:

\[
J = s v_{sub}
\]

Solving for velocity:

\[
v_{sub} = \frac{J}{s}
\]

---

## Proposed Gravitational Coupling

Assume force density proportional to moving information density:

\[
F_{grav} = k s v_{sub}
\]

Where:

k = gravitational coupling constant.

---

## Radial Flux Conservation

For spherical symmetry:

\[
\nabla \cdot J = 0
\]

This gives:

\[
r^2 s v_r = C
\]

---

## Velocity Field

\[
v_r = \frac{C}{r^2 s}
\]

---

## Force Law

Substituting into the coupling relation:

\[
F_{grav} = k s v_r
\]

\[
F_{grav} = \frac{kC}{r^2}
\]

---

## Result

An inverse-square force law emerges:

\[
F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}
\]

This is mathematically consistent with Newton-type gravitational scaling.

---

## Interpretation

Gravity can be interpreted as the interaction between:

- information density
- substrate flow
- conservation of information flux
