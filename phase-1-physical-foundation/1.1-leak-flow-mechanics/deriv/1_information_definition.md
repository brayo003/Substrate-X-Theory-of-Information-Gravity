# 1. Information Density Definition

## Total Information in a Volume

Let the total information contained in a spatial region \(V\) be:

\[
I = \int_V s(x,t)\, dV
\]

Where:

- \(I\) = total information
- \(s(x,t)\) = information density

---

## Definition of Information Density

\[
s(x,t) = \frac{dI}{dV}
\]

Units (conceptual):

\[
[s] = \text{information} / \text{volume}
\]

Example units:

info/m³

---

## Interpretation

Information density measures how much information exists locally within the substrate.

Spatial variation of information density produces gradients:

\[
\nabla s
\]

These gradients will later drive transport and forces.

---

## Role in Substrate-X Theory

Information density is the **primary field variable**.

All dynamical behavior in the framework emerges from:

- transport of information
- generation of information
- destruction of information
