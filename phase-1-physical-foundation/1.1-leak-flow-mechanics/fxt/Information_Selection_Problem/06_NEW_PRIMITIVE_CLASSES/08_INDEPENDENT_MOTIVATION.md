## NC-1 — Constraint Propagation (Cotton-York)

**Independent motivation:**

The Cotton-York tensor Y^{ij} appears naturally in:
- York's (1973) canonical decomposition of GR initial data — independently motivated by the conformal thin-sandwich problem
- Conformal gravity (Weyl-squared action in 3+1D) — the EOM involve the Cotton-York tensor
- The extrinsic curvature decomposition in numerical relativity (BSSN formulation)

The condition Y^{ij} = 0 corresponds to "conformally flat extrinsic curvature" — a natural gauge condition in certain formulations. It has non-cosmological motivation from numerical relativity and canonical decomposition theory.

**Verdict: WEAK INDEPENDENT MOTIVATION.** The condition has technical uses in GR but is not a fundamental physical principle independent of R1. Its non-cosmological applications are methodological (gauge choices, decomposition) rather than physical (derived constraints on nature).

## NC-2 — Quantum Extension Completeness

**Independent motivation:**

The requirement that initial data admit a non-singular quantum extension is implicitly assumed in every quantum cosmology calculation — LQC, CDT, spin-foam cosmology all assume they are working with configurations that have a quantum completion. This is independently motivated by: the desire for a consistent quantum cosmological theory, the resolution of the big bang singularity as a physical requirement, the practical need for initial conditions that can be placed at a finite Planck epoch.

**Verdict: MODERATE INDEPENDENT MOTIVATION.** The condition is implicitly assumed in all quantum cosmology. It is not constructed for R1. However, it does not produce a new selection beyond what existing quantum cosmology already assumes.
