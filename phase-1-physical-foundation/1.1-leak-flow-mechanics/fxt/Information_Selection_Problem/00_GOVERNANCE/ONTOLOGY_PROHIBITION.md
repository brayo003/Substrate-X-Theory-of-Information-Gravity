# ONTOLOGY_PROHIBITION
## Prohibited Names, Concepts, and Structures

---

### Purpose

This document lists names, concepts, and ontological structures that are **prohibited from use in this workspace** until they are **earned** by derivation from permitted inputs.

The prohibition is not semantic. It is structural. A concept is prohibited not because its name is ugly, but because using it prematurely forecloses the derivation. It introduces a conclusion as vocabulary.

---

### The Core Prohibition Rule

Any name that **prejudges the mathematical status of the unknown** is prohibited until derivation forces that status.

The unknown — whatever closes the residual gap, if one exists — must arrive at its name through derivation. It gets whatever mathematical status the derivation forces upon it. It is not named in advance.

The following equalities/equivalences are prohibited as **assumed** inputs. They may appear as **derived outputs** only.

### Section D: The Earning Protocol

A prohibited concept **earns its name** by the following process:

1. A derivation produces a mathematical object X with properties P₁, P₂, ..., Pₙ.
2. An adversarial examination confirms X is not a disguised version of a prohibited concept (all Section A and B items checked).
3. X is shown to be the **minimum structure** required to close the identified residual.
4. X is given a **provisional name** (e.g., "the Q-field" or "the R-constraint") that is descriptive of its mathematical properties, not its presumed physical interpretation.
5. Only after (4) is established can the physical interpretation be proposed — and it remains tentative until experimental discrimination.

The purpose is to prevent the vocabulary from running ahead of the mathematics.
