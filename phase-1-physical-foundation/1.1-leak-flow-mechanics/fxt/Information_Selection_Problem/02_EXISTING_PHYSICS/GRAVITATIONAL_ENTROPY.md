# GRAVITATIONAL_ENTROPY
## Gate 2 — Known Proposals for Gravitational Entropy

---

### Purpose

Catalog all known proposals for defining gravitational entropy, assess their validity and scope, and determine which (if any) establishes the low-entropy character of the early universe on solid ground.

This document directly addresses the Gate 1 finding that Weyl = gravitational entropy is a contested conjecture.

---

## Section 1: Why Gravitational Entropy Is Hard

Gravitational systems are pathological for standard entropy:
1. Gravity is attractive and long-range — no thermodynamic limit in the usual sense
2. No ground state bounded below — the more matter clusters, the lower the gravitational energy
3. Black hole entropy (Bekenstein-Hawking: S_BH = A/4G) is established but derived from thermodynamic analogies, not microscopic state-counting (except in special string-theoretic cases)
4. The space of GR initial data has no obvious natural measure

All proposed gravitational entropy measures are therefore heuristic, approximate, or derived from specific limits.

---

## Section 2: The Weyl Curvature Hypothesis (Penrose)

**The Proposal:** Gravitational entropy is measured by Weyl curvature invariants:
```
S_grav ∝ C_μνρσ C^μνρσ  (schematically)
```
Early universe: C_μνρσ → 0 (low entropy). Late universe (black holes): C_μνρσ → ∞ (high entropy).

**Arguments in its favor:** Qualitatively captures the asymmetry. FLRW has C_μνρσ = 0 identically. Connects to collapse irreversibility.

**Published criticisms:**

a) **Normalization:** C_μνρσ C^μνρσ = 0 for all conformally flat spacetimes, including Minkowski. Zero value is not unique to low-entropy cosmological states.

b) **Bianchi counterexamples (Clifton, Ellis, Tavakoli 2013):** Anisotropic Bianchi models can have small Weyl invariants while being highly inhomogeneous. The invariant does not fully capture disorder.

c) **No microscopic derivation:** No state-counting argument gives S ∝ C_μνρσ C^μνρσ.

d) **Dimensional problem:** C_μνρσ C^μνρσ has dimensions [length]^{-4}. Entropy must be dimensionless. Requires additional structure (G, ℏ, c) not specified.

e) **Conformal ambiguity:** Conformally related spacetimes have different Weyl tensors. If entropy is not conformally invariant, which frame is physical?

**Status: CONJECTURAL — not derived, contains counterexamples, dimensional gap.**

---

## Section 3: Bekenstein-Hawking Entropy

**The Result:** S_BH = kc³A/(4Gℏ) for a black hole with area A. Most solidly established result in gravitational thermodynamics.

**Application to cosmological initial state:** No black holes in the early universe → S_BH ≈ 0. Consistent with low early gravitational entropy, but not explanatory. The formula applies to stationary black holes; extension to cosmological spacetimes is contested.

**Status: ESTABLISHED for black holes, CONTESTED for cosmological application.**

## Section 4: Other Proposals

| Proposal | Description | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Covariant entropy bound (Bousso) | S ≤ A/4G on null surfaces | Bound, not definition; does not explain selection |
| Morphological information entropy (Buchert et al.) | Entropy of matter distribution morphology | Captures matter sector only |
| Gravitational Gibbs entropy | Thermodynamic entropy for gravitational field | Ill-defined: no bounded Hamiltonian |
| Holographic screen entropy | Entropy on holographic screens | Applies in specific limits, not general |

## Section 5: Entropy Scales of the Observable Universe

| Component | Entropy (k_B units) |
|---|---|
| CMB photons | ~10^{88} |
| Stellar black holes | ~10^{97} |
| Supermassive black holes | ~10^{103} |
| Penrose's estimated final state | ~10^{123} |

The early universe entropy was ~10^{88} (radiation-dominated). Current entropy is ~10^{103} (black hole dominated). The ~10^{15} orders of magnitude difference in the black hole sector is the gravitational entropy growth that requires explanation.

**These estimates depend on Penrose's framework. Under other entropy definitions the numbers change.**

---

## Gate 2 Verdict for Gravitational Entropy

**Conclusion: There is no established, derivation-backed entropy for the gravitational degrees of freedom of a general cosmological spacetime.**

The Weyl curvature conjecture is the best available proposal but is unproven and contested. The Bekenstein-Hawking result applies to black holes. No framework covers the gravitational DOF of smooth early-universe geometry.

This gap is itself part of the residual: the initial-state problem cannot be stated precisely until a valid gravitational entropy measure is established.
