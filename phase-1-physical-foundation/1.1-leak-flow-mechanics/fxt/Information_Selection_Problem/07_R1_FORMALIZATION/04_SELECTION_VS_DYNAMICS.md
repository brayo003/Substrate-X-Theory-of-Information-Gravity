# 04_SELECTION_VS_DYNAMICS
## Initial-State Selection vs. Dynamical Selection: Formal Distinction

**Date:** August 2026

---

## The Seven Categories

**Category 1 — Initial-state selection:**
A mathematical structure that specifies which initial-data configurations (h_ij, K_ij) are physically realizable, independently of the subsequent dynamical evolution. The result is Γ_phys ⊊ Γ_GR at t = t_initial.

**Category 2 — Dynamical attraction:**
A dynamical law such that, for initial data in a wide basin B ⊆ Γ_GR, the solution at time t > t_initial approaches a specific target configuration (e.g., FLRW). The initial data is NOT restricted to Γ_phys ⊊ Γ_GR — instead, evolution maps a large set of initial conditions toward the target.

Example: Ekpyrotic slow-contraction attractor. Starting from highly inhomogeneous data (within the basin), the solution becomes smooth and nearly FLRW before the bounce.

**Category 3 — Quantum-state selection:**
A specific quantum state |Ψ⟩ of the gravitational field is selected by a physical principle. The semiclassical configurations supported by |Ψ⟩ define an effective Γ_phys — but Γ_phys is derived from |Ψ⟩, not defined independently.

Example: Hartle-Hawking no-boundary proposal. Ψ[h] = ∫_{no past boundary} Dg exp(−S_E). This selects a quantum state; the support of |Ψ|² in configuration space defines the effective initial conditions.

**Category 4 — Path-integral weighting:**
The path integral over geometries assigns different amplitudes to different configurations. Configurations with small action have larger amplitude. This is a continuous weighting, not a selection: every configuration has nonzero amplitude.

Example: P01 (asymptotic safety). exp(−β∫C²√g) gives FLRW amplitude 1 and Kasner amplitude ~0.9. This is a weighting, not a selection.

**Category 5 — Boundary-condition selection:**
A boundary condition imposed on the past boundary of the spacetime restricts which initial configurations are permitted. The boundary condition is a stipulation.

Example: The no-boundary proposal stipulates no past boundary. This is a boundary condition, not a derivation.

**Category 6 — Probabilistic typicality:**
A measure on Γ_GR assigns probability to each configuration. The observed initial state is described as "typical" or "probable" under this measure. This requires a defined measure and an explanation of why that measure is the correct one.

Example: Penrose's 10^{-10^{123}} estimate uses the Boltzmann entropy counting measure. This requires the Penrose WCH (S_grav ~ C²) and a specific state space.

**Category 7 — Emergent-state selection:**
The initial state emerges from a pre-geometric or discrete substrate. The "initial state" is not a fundamental object — it is the low-energy limit of a more fundamental description. The selection is performed at the level of the discrete substrate, and the smooth GR initial state is its consequence.

Example: GFT condensate cosmology. The FLRW equation emerges from a condensate state of GFT. But the condensate state is assumed, not derived.

## Logical Distinctness

**Is R1 asking about Category 1?**

R1 as currently stated: "why did the gravitational degrees of freedom occupy an extremely small region of their available phase space."

The phrase "available phase space" implies the full Γ_GR was available and only a small subset was realized. This is Category 1 language — initial-state selection.

However, the phrase "no derivation from independent premises exists" is epistemological — it states that no mechanism has been found, not that no mechanism could exist. This framing is compatible with Categories 1–7 as potential answers.

**Can Categories 2–7 solve R1 without relocating the question?**

- **Category 2 (dynamical attraction):** Relocates R1 to "why was the initial data in the attractor basin?" This is a genuine shift (ekpyrosis shifts R1 to the pre-contraction state) but not a closure. Category 2 alone cannot close R1 — it always requires an upstream initial condition.

- **Category 3 (quantum-state selection):** Can close R1 IF the quantum state is independently derived (not stipulated). No such derivation exists for any gravity theory (AS_QUANTUM_STATE_AUDIT.md). Requires specifying Ψ without the state depending on the desired outcome — which is the challenge not solved by any existing mechanism.

- **Category 4 (path-integral weighting):** Produces Outcome B (weighting), not Outcome A (restriction). Cannot close R1 unless the weighting is exponentially strong (which it is not for any known mechanism, established in Gate 6).

- **Category 5 (boundary condition):** Stipulates rather than derives. Cannot close R1 — it relocates the question to "why this boundary condition?"

- **Category 6 (probabilistic typicality):** Requires a defined measure and the measure itself requires justification. Without a canonical measure (established in 02_MEASURE_AUDIT.md), Category 6 is not well-defined for Γ_GR.

- **Category 7 (emergent state):** Can close R1 IF the emergent state is derived from the substrate with a specific selection mechanism. No existing framework does this without importing the state as input.

## The Implication for R1

**R1 is specifically about Category 1 — initial-state selection.** It asks why the initial state was in the restricted sector, not merely that dynamics later produced a smooth universe.

A solution via Category 2 (dynamics) is legitimate ONLY if it can demonstrate:
- The basin of attraction covers all or most of Γ_GR (not just a pre-selected region)
- The initial condition for the attractor is itself derived (not just assumed to be "generic")

Ekpyrosis partially satisfies the first condition (wide basin for slow contraction) but fails the second (pre-contraction state is not derived). This is the ekpyrotic gap from Gate 4.

A solution via Category 3 (quantum state) is legitimate ONLY if:
- The quantum state is derived from first principles without importing the desired initial condition as input

No existing mechanism provides this.

**Conclusion:** R1 is asking for Category 1 (initial-state selection) or a Category 3 solution (quantum-state selection from first principles). Categories 4–6 are insufficient. Category 2 can shift but not close R1.
