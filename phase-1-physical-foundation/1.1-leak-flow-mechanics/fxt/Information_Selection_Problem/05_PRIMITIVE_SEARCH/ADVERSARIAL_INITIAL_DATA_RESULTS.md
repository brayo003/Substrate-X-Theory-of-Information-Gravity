## The Seven Adversarial Classes

For each survivor, the primitive is evaluated against seven initial-data classes. The goal is not to show that the primitive "prefers smooth states" — it is to derive WHY the primitive assigns a different value, status, or verdict to each class.

A primitive that assigns the same status to all seven classes has not restricted Γ_GR. A primitive that restricts only classes 2–7 but not class 1 has done the required work.

**Class D1 — FLRW-like smooth data**
(h_ij = a²δ_ij, K_ij = Hδ_ij with H = ȧ/a; Weyl tensor C_{αβγδ} = 0 exactly; homogeneous, isotropic)
This is the target state. A primitive that excludes D1 has failed.

**Class D2 — Bianchi I anisotropic data**
(h_ij = diag(a₁², a₂², a₃²); K_ij = diag(H₁, H₂, H₃) with H_i distinct; Weyl ≠ 0 via electric part E_{ij} = σ_{ij} ≠ 0 where σ_{ij} is the shear tensor)
Homogeneous but anisotropic. Weyl curvature is generated by shear σ_{ij}. The shear scalar σ² = σ_{ij}σ^{ij} is a direct measure of Weyl content.

**Class D3 — Bianchi IX / mixmaster data**
(h_ij governed by three independent scale factors with non-commuting structure constants of SO(3); K_ij includes off-diagonal terms; BKL oscillations near a singularity produce chaotic mixmaster behavior)
Homogeneous, non-isotropic, large oscillatory Weyl curvature. Generic behavior near a singularity in GR.

**Class D4 — Highly inhomogeneous data**
(h_ij arbitrary Riemannian metric with spatial gradients of order ∂_k h_ij ~ L_UV^{-1} where L_UV is the UV cutoff scale; K_ij general; Weyl curvature varies on scale L_UV)
Neither homogeneous nor isotropic. Representative of a generic random gravitational field configuration.

**Class D5 — Large-Weyl data**
(h_ij such that the Weyl scalar W² = C_{αβγδ}C^{αβγδ} >> 1 in Planck units; can be constructed explicitly as superposition of linearized gravitational wave modes with large amplitude or as Kasner solution with large anisotropy exponents)
Specifically designed to have large Weyl curvature while otherwise satisfying GR constraint equations.

**Class D6 — Gravitationally clumped data**
(h_ij = Schwarzschild slice: ds² = (1 - 2GM/r)^{-1}dr² + r²dΩ²; K_ij = 0 (time-symmetric slice); large Weyl curvature concentrated near r = 2GM; total mass M >> M_Planck)
A single black hole initial-data slice. Maximum Weyl curvature at the horizon; Weyl ~ M/r³ far from the hole.

**Class D7 — Multi-black-hole clumped data**
(h_ij = Brill-Lindquist multi-black-hole initial data: ψ⁴δ_ij where ψ = 1 + ∑_i M_i/2|x - x_i|; K_ij = 0; total Weyl curvature ~ ∑_i M_i²/r_i⁶)
Multiple black holes. Representative of a thermalized gravitational state — maximal Weyl curvature consistent with finite total ADM mass.

## P01 — Asymptotic Safety UV Fixed-Point Restriction

**The primitive:** Physical initial data lies on a renormalized trajectory in theory space that emanates from the UV fixed point G* of the gravitational RG flow. In the semi-classical limit, this translates to a restriction on which field configurations are produced by the UV-complete quantum gravity theory.

**The proposed distinction mechanism:** In the asymptotic safety program, the effective average action Γ_k satisfies the FRGE (functional renormalization group equation). At the UV fixed point G*, the dimensionless couplings g* = G·k² and λ* = Λ/k² take specific values. The renormalized trajectory flowing from G* to k → 0 (the infrared) traces out the physical theory. Initial-data configurations that are NOT on this trajectory correspond to theories that do not flow from G* — they require irrelevant couplings (in the RG sense) to be nonzero.

**Class D1 — FLRW smooth data:**
The Reuter fixed point in truncated theory space (Einstein-Hilbert truncation) gives G* ~ 0.27 and λ* ~ 0.19 (Lauscher-Reuter 2002 values in appropriate units). The renormalized trajectory flows from G* through the Planck scale toward the classical GR region. In the classical limit, the trajectory produces standard GR dynamics — which includes FLRW solutions. D1 data is compatible with the renormalized trajectory.
**Status: PERMITTED by P01.** The trajectory from G* produces standard GR in the classical limit, and FLRW is a solution of standard GR.

**Class D2 — Bianchi I anisotropic data:**
Bianchi I is also a solution of standard GR (the Kasner solution is a Bianchi I vacuum solution). The renormalized trajectory flows to classical GR, which admits Bianchi I solutions. P01 does not directly exclude Bianchi I from the classical trajectory.
**Critical assessment:** The UV fixed point restricts theory space (the coupling constants), not the space of classical solutions. Every solution of classical GR, including Bianchi I, is on the renormalized trajectory in the sense that it satisfies the classical equations of motion. P01 does NOT distinguish D1 from D2 unless the UV fixed point imposes additional constraints beyond the classical equations.

The known asymptotic safety literature does not establish that the UV critical manifold excludes Kasner/Bianchi I solutions. Platania (2020) and Bonanno-Reuter (2002) study asymptotic safety cosmology but restrict to FLRW backgrounds (minisuperspace truncation).
**Status: AMBIGUOUS — P01 as stated does not exclude D2.**

**Class D3 — Bianchi IX mixmaster data:**
Same logic as D2. Bianchi IX is a classical GR solution. The RG trajectory flows to standard GR, which permits Bianchi IX. No mechanism in asymptotic safety literature specifically excludes mixmaster initial data.
**Status: AMBIGUOUS — P01 does not exclude D3.**

**Class D4 — Highly inhomogeneous data:**
Here P01 may have traction. In the asymptotic safety effective action, highly inhomogeneous configurations with curvature approaching the Planck scale have large contributions from higher-derivative operators (R², RμνRμν, ...) with coefficients set by the RG flow. At the fixed point, these contributions are controlled by the fixed-point values of the higher-derivative couplings. Reuter-Saueressig (2002) show that near G*, the effective action contains an infinite series of higher-derivative corrections that suppress configurations with curvature scales above k ~ M_Planck.

However: the suppression is of configurations with R ~ M_Planck², not specifically of configurations with large Weyl curvature. The asymptotic safety fixed point suppresses all high-curvature configurations, including Ricci-dominated ones (which are not the problematic class for R1).
**Status: PARTIAL — P01 suppresses high-total-curvature data but does not specifically distinguish high-Weyl from high-Ricci.**

**Class D5 — Large-Weyl data:**
Same issue as D4. The asymptotic safety effective action suppresses high-curvature configurations in the curvature invariants R², RμνRμν, and CμνρσCμνρσ. The last term specifically suppresses large Weyl curvature. If the fixed-point effective action contains a positive coefficient for CμνρσCμνρσ, this would specifically suppress large-Weyl initial data.

Checking the literature: In the R + R² truncation (Lauscher-Reuter 2002), the coefficient of R² at the fixed point is positive — this suppresses large Ricci scalar. In the R + CμνρσCμνρσ truncation (Falls-Litim 2014; Ohta-Percacci 2016), the coefficient of the Weyl-squared term at the fixed point is also positive. This means that large-Weyl configurations are suppressed relative to smooth ones at the fixed point.

This is a genuine, derived distinction — not an assumed one. The suppression follows from the fixed-point values of the Weyl-squared coupling, which are computed from the FRGE independently of any cosmological assumption.
**Status: SUPPRESSED by P01 in the large-j limit, with independent derivation from fixed-point couplings.**

**Class D6 — Black hole (Schwarzschild) data:**
A Schwarzschild black hole has Weyl tensor C ~ M/r³. The asymptotic safety corrections near the black hole singularity are studied in Bonanno-Reuter (2000) — they find the singularity is resolved and the effective metric near r=0 resembles a de Sitter core. The fixed-point structure regularizes the singularity, reducing the Weyl curvature divergence.

However, a macroscopic black hole with M >> M_Planck is far from the UV fixed point — the RG flow has already run far toward the IR. For such data, the UV fixed-point restriction does not actively suppress the configuration. The black hole data is a classical GR solution and not excluded by the UV fixed-point condition in any established calculation.
**Status: NOT EXCLUDED by P01 for macroscopic black holes (M >> M_Planck).**

**Class D7 — Multi-black-hole data:**
Same as D6. Multi-black-hole configurations with macroscopic masses are classical GR solutions far from the UV fixed point. Not excluded.
**Status: NOT EXCLUDED by P01.**

**P01 ADVERSARIAL VERDICT:**
P01 achieves partial distinction. It suppresses large-Weyl configurations at the Planck scale via the positive Weyl-squared coupling at the fixed point (D5). It does NOT exclude Bianchi I, Bianchi IX, or macroscopic black holes (D2, D3, D6, D7). The distinction it provides is:

- Configurations with curvature near or above M_Planck² and large Weyl are suppressed.
- Classical-regime configurations (sub-Planckian curvature) are not distinguished — all solutions of classical GR are compatible with the UV trajectory.

**Fundamental limitation identified:** P01 restricts the UV regime (k ~ M_Planck) but not the space of classical solutions in the IR. Since R1 asks about the initial state of our observable universe — which, even at the Planck epoch, may be in the classical or semi-classical regime — P01's restriction applies only at the very highest energy scales and does not address the full space of classically allowed initial data.

**Survival status after adversarial test:** CONDITIONAL — the mechanism provides a Planck-scale suppression of large-Weyl configurations but does not fully restrict the classical initial-data space. R1 requires restricting the full space of classically allowed initial data, not only trans-Planckian configurations.

## P02 — Local Rindler Horizon Thermodynamic Constraint

**The primitive:** For any local Rindler horizon in spacetime, the thermodynamic identity δQ = TdS (with T = ℏa/2π and S = A/4G) holds. This Clausius-relation consistency condition must be satisfied everywhere in spacetime, including at the initial slice.

**The proposed distinction mechanism:** High-Weyl initial data produces strong focusing of null geodesics via the Raychaudhuri equation. If the focusing is strong enough, it generates caustics (θ → -∞ in finite affine parameter). At a caustic, the area element of a null congruence goes to zero, violating the thermodynamic accounting (S = A/4G → 0 while physical entropy flux remains finite). This is the proposed mechanism for excluding high-Weyl initial data.

**The Raychaudhuri equation:** dθ/dλ = -θ²/2 - σ_{ab}σ^{ab} - R_{ab}l^al^b + ω_{ab}ω^{ab}

For a hypersurface-orthogonal congruence (ω_{ab} = 0):
dθ/dλ = -θ²/2 - σ_{ab}σ^{ab} - R_{ab}l^al^b

The Weyl curvature contributes to R_{ab}l^al^b via the decomposition R_{ab} = (R/4)g_{ab} + S_{ab} where S_{ab} is the traceless Ricci. The electric Weyl tensor E_{ab} = C_{acbd}l^cl^d contributes to the shear evolution but not directly to θ̇ via the Raychaudhuri equation. However, the Weyl tensor drives tidal distortion, which through σ_{ab}σ^{ab} contributes to θ̇ indirectly.

**Class D1 — FLRW smooth data:**
In FLRW, the Weyl tensor vanishes identically: C_{αβγδ} = 0. The shear σ_{ab} = 0. The Raychaudhuri equation reduces to dθ/dλ = -θ²/2 - R_{ab}l^al^b. For a matter source satisfying the null energy condition (R_{ab}l^al^b ≥ 0), the expansion is non-positive and monotonically decreasing — null geodesics focus but in a controlled manner consistent with thermodynamic accounting (the area theorem holds for null congruences in matter satisfying NEC).

The thermodynamic identity holds in FLRW. Local Rindler horizons in FLRW are well-defined, and Jacobson's (1995) argument was applied specifically to recover the Friedmann equations from the thermodynamic identity by Cai-Kim (2005).
**Status: PERMITTED — thermodynamic identity holds in FLRW.**

**Class D2 — Bianchi I anisotropic data:**
In Bianchi I (Kasner vacuum), R_{ab} = 0 (vacuum), but shear σ_{ab} ≠ 0. The Weyl tensor is generated by the anisotropy via E_{ab} = σ_{ab}σ_c^b - (1/3)σ²δ_{ab} (schematically). The Raychaudhuri equation gives dθ/dλ = -θ²/2 - σ_{ab}σ^{ab}.

The shear contributes a negative term, accelerating focusing. For large shear (σ² >> 1 in Planck units), null congruences focus rapidly. Whether this rapid focusing violates the thermodynamic identity depends on whether the thermodynamic entropy flux through the horizon remains finite when the area goes to zero.

Specific calculation: In Kasner, a_{ij}(t) = t^{2p_i}δ_{ij} with ∑p_i = 1, ∑p_i² = 1. The shear σ² = (2/3)∑_{i<j}(H_i - H_j)²/9 where H_i = ṗ_i/t. For the most anisotropic Kasner (p₁ = 1, p₂ = p₃ = 0): σ² ~ 1/t². A null congruence in this background focuses at t → 0 (the singularity). The thermodynamic entropy S = A/4G → 0 at the singularity.

Does this violate the Clausius relation? The entropy flux into the local horizon must also go to zero as the area goes to zero, unless there is a flux divergence. In Kasner, the energy density of matter is zero (vacuum), so the entropy flux is from gravitational waves only. Whether gravitational entropy flux through a Rindler horizon in Kasner remains finite while the horizon area goes to zero requires a calculation not done in the existing literature.

**Critical assessment:** The mechanism is not definitively established for Bianchi I. The focusing in Bianchi I is faster than in FLRW, but whether it specifically violates the thermodynamic identity (and not merely approaches a singularity in a controlled way) is not proven.
**Status: UNRESOLVED — the mechanism may or may not distinguish D2 from D1.**

**Class D3 — Bianchi IX mixmaster data:**
Bianchi IX has chaotic BKL oscillations near the singularity. The shear oscillates with growing amplitude: σ² ~ 1/t². Multiple null congruences focus and refocus with rapidly varying rates. The Kasner epochs alternate chaotically. The thermodynamic identity faces a more complex challenge here because the focusing/defocusing alternation may produce net entropy violations in the accounting.

However, the same fundamental problem as D2 applies: it is not established in the literature that the Clausius relation specifically excludes mixmaster initial data.
**Status: UNRESOLVED.**

**Class D4 — Highly inhomogeneous data:**
In a highly inhomogeneous spacetime, local Rindler horizons have complicated structure. The proposed mechanism is that in regions with large local Weyl curvature, the null congruences focus too rapidly for the thermodynamic accounting to hold. This is physically reasonable but quantitatively unestablished.
**Status: UNRESOLVED.**

**Class D5 — Large-Weyl data:**
This is the class where P02 is designed to act. Large Weyl → large shear via E_{ab} → large |dθ/dλ| → rapid focusing → rapid area decrease. If the area shrinks faster than the entropy flux decreases, the Clausius relation is violated.

Quantitative check: Consider a null congruence in a spacetime with C_{αβγδ} ~ C₀ >> 1 (Planck units). The shear evolution equation: dσ_{ab}/dλ = -θσ_{ab} - (σ²)σ_{ab}/2 + C_{abcd}l^cl^d (schematically). For C₀ >> 1, the shear grows rapidly, driving dθ/dλ to be large and negative, causing θ → -∞ in affine parameter Δλ ~ 1/√C₀. The horizon area shrinks to zero in Δλ ~ 1/√C₀ Planck lengths. The thermodynamic temperature T = ℏa/2π where a is the proper acceleration of a local Rindler observer — for a near the focusing point, the time to reach the caustic is finite and small. Whether the entropy balance closes in this time depends on the matter content and coupling.

This is a genuine mechanism in the right direction but requires a detailed calculation to establish definitively.
**Status: PARTIAL — plausible mechanism exists but quantitative demonstration is absent.**

**Class D6 — Schwarzschild data:**
The Schwarzschild metric has a well-defined Rindler horizon structure: the event horizon itself functions as a Rindler horizon for a stationary observer. Hawking radiation from this horizon is thermodynamically consistent — S = A/4G and T = ℏ/(8πGM) satisfy the first law. P02 does not exclude Schwarzschild data; it is explicitly thermodynamically consistent.
**Status: PERMITTED — Schwarzschild data is thermodynamically consistent.**

**Class D7 — Multi-black-hole data:**
Same as D6. Each black hole carries thermodynamic entropy A/4G. The multi-black-hole configuration is thermodynamically consistent at the level of individual horizons.
**Status: PERMITTED — multi-BH data is thermodynamically consistent.**

**P02 ADVERSARIAL VERDICT:**
P02 permits D1, D6, D7. It does not definitively exclude D2, D3, D4. It provides a plausible (unproven) mechanism for D5. The primitive fails to distinguish FLRW from Bianchi I anisotropic data — which is a critical failure, since Bianchi I has non-zero Weyl curvature and should be excluded.

The fundamental problem: The Clausius relation δQ = TdS produces the Einstein equations (Jacobson 1995) — meaning any solution of GR's equations of motion satisfies the thermodynamic identity by construction. Bianchi I and Bianchi IX are exact vacuum solutions of GR; they satisfy the thermodynamic identity because they satisfy Einstein's equations. P02 cannot exclude solutions of GR using a condition that is equivalent to (and derived from) GR's equations of motion.

**This is a decisive result.** The Jacobson derivation shows that the thermodynamic identity is equivalent to Einstein's equations. Therefore, any configuration satisfying Einstein's equations satisfies P02 — including all Bianchi models, Kasner solutions, and mixmaster data. P02 cannot restrict to a subset of GR solutions.

**Survival status after adversarial test: KILLED.** The thermodynamic identity (P02) is equivalent to Einstein's equations and is satisfied by all solutions of GR. It cannot restrict the space of GR solutions to the low-Weyl sector.

**Exact kill reason:** P02 fails the adversarial test decisively. The Clausius relation δQ = TdS for local Rindler horizons is equivalent to the Einstein equation G_{ab} = 8πG T_{ab} (Jacobson 1995). Any spacetime satisfying Einstein's equations satisfies P02. Bianchi I, Bianchi IX, Kasner, and all other exact GR solutions therefore satisfy P02. The primitive does not distinguish FLRW from any other GR solution. It cannot restrict Γ_GR.

## P06 — Constraint-Algebra Anomaly-Freedom

**The primitive:** The physical Hilbert space H_phys consists of states annihilated by all quantum gravitational constraints without producing an anomalous commutator: [Ĥ[N], Ĥ[M]] - Ĥ[{N,M}] = anomaly. Anomaly-free quantization requires the anomaly to vanish on physical states.

**The proposed distinction mechanism:** In the full (non-minisuperspace) quantization of GR, the constraint algebra has structure functions that depend on the spatial metric h_{ij}. Specifically: {H(N), H(M)} = H_i(h^{ij}(N∂_jM - M∂_jN)). The right-hand side depends on h^{ij} — this is what makes it a Lie algebroid with structure functions rather than a Lie algebra. Upon quantization, the metric h^{ij} becomes an operator, and the commutator of two Hamiltonian constraints acquires quantum corrections that may not equal the quantum version of the right-hand side — this is the anomaly.

**Class D1 — FLRW smooth data:**
In the minisuperspace quantization (LQC), the anomaly-free physical states include the standard LQC bounce solution, which connects a contracting FLRW to an expanding FLRW. The quantum state corresponding to smooth FLRW data is in the anomaly-free sector (it was specifically designed to be so in LQC). In the full theory context, FLRW states are expected to be in the anomaly-free sector because the FLRW algebra is a sub-algebra of the full constraint algebra.
**Status: PERMITTED.**

**Class D2 — Bianchi I anisotropic data:**
The Bianchi I quantization in LQC has also been studied (Ashtekar-Wilson-Ewing 2009). The Bianchi I quantum constraint algebra has been analyzed for anomaly freedom. The result: anomaly-free states exist for Bianchi I, and they correspond to the classical Bianchi I solutions in the semi-classical limit. Bianchi I is NOT excluded by anomaly freedom.
**Status: PERMITTED — anomaly-free Bianchi I states exist in LQC.**

**Class D3 — Bianchi IX mixmaster data:**
The Bianchi IX quantum cosmology has been studied (e.g., Bojowald-Date 2002). Finding anomaly-free states is technically difficult, but the problem has not been shown to be impossible. The BKL oscillations make the quantum theory harder to handle, but there is no established proof that Bianchi IX data is excluded from the anomaly-free sector.
**Status: UNRESOLVED.**

**Class D4 — Highly inhomogeneous data:**
For general inhomogeneous data, the full quantum constraint algebra has never been constructed without anomaly. In perturbative approaches (Fernandez-Mendez et al. 2012; Gomar et al. 2015), anomaly cancellation is enforced perturbatively in inhomogeneous extensions of LQC. These results are not established in the full theory.

The critical question: Does anomaly freedom in the full theory select smooth (low-Weyl) initial data specifically? This is the key claim, but it is not established. What is known: anomaly-free quantization places restrictions on the state, but whether those restrictions specifically exclude high-Weyl configurations is unknown.
**Status: UNKNOWN.**

**Class D5 — Large-Weyl data:**
Unknown. The connection between the Weyl tensor of the classical initial data and the anomaly structure of the quantum constraint algebra is not established. It could be that large-Weyl configurations generate anomalies in the quantum theory — but this has not been demonstrated.
**Status: UNKNOWN.**

**Class D6 — Schwarzschild data:**
Black hole quantization in LQG (Thiemann-Kastrup 1993; Modesto 2006) produces anomaly-free quantum states. Schwarzschild data is not excluded by anomaly freedom.
**Status: PERMITTED.**

**Class D7 — Multi-black-hole data:**
No analysis available, but by extension of D6, likely permitted.
**Status: LIKELY PERMITTED.**

**P06 ADVERSARIAL VERDICT:**
P06 does not distinguish D1 from D2. Anomaly-free Bianchi I states exist in LQC. This is a critical failure of the same type as P02 — the primitive was designed to constrain the space of physical states, but the constraint is satisfied by anisotropic solutions.

For D4 and D5, the status is unknown — there may be a mechanism, but it has not been demonstrated. The unknown status means the primitive cannot be used to establish a restriction on Γ_GR.

**The deeper problem:** Anomaly cancellation in LQC is achieved precisely by constructing quantum states for every classical Bianchi model that is studied. The anomaly-free condition is a consistency requirement of the quantization, not a selector of specific classical geometries. It is satisfied by any quantization that is done correctly — meaning researchers construct anomaly-free theories for Bianchi I, Bianchi IX, etc., by design.

**Survival status after adversarial test: CONDITIONAL-WEAK.** P06 does not exclude Bianchi I (D2), which is a critical adversarial class. It may distinguish D4/D5 (highly inhomogeneous and large-Weyl data) in the full theory, but this is not established. The mechanism is active only in the quantum regime where full-theory quantum GR is available — which it is not.

P06 is not killed but is severely weakened. It cannot establish Γ_phys ⊊ Γ_GR for the classical regime.

## P08 — Spin-Foam Vertex-Amplitude Curvature Suppression

**The primitive:** The EPRL spin-foam transition amplitude W(s,s') = ∑_σ ∏_v A_v ∏_f A_f has a vertex amplitude A_v that in the large-j (semiclassical) limit is: A_v ~ exp(i·S_Regge) where S_Regge = ∑_f A_f θ_f is the Regge action summed over triangle areas A_f and dihedral angles θ_f (the discrete curvature). Physical geometries are those that are stationary-phase saddle points of the spin-foam sum: δS_Regge = 0.

**The proposed distinction mechanism:** The Regge action S_Regge is stationary when all dihedral angles θ_f satisfy the Regge equations (discrete Einstein equations). Away from the saddle, the amplitude oscillates rapidly and cancels. The width of the saddle around a given classical geometry is controlled by the second variation δ²S_Regge — this gives a Gaussian suppression of configurations deviating from the classical saddle. For large deviations (large curvature), the suppression is exponential in j.

**Class D1 — FLRW smooth data:**
The FLRW geometry corresponds to a de Sitter or flat Regge geometry where all dihedral angles satisfy the flat-space condition (θ_f = θ_f^{flat}). This is the dominant saddle of the EPRL amplitude in cosmological spin-foam models. It is a stationary point of S_Regge. The amplitude is maximal here.
**Status: DOMINANT SADDLE — D1 is the dominant contribution to the spin-foam sum.**

**Class D2 — Bianchi I anisotropic data:**
Bianchi I (Kasner) has a non-trivial Regge representation: the simplicial geometry corresponding to Kasner has anisotropic dihedral angles θ_f that satisfy the Kasner Regge equations (discrete vacuum Einstein equations for the anisotropic case). This IS a saddle of S_Regge — the Kasner solution satisfies Einstein's equations and therefore also the Regge equations in the simplicial approximation.

The EPRL amplitude in the large-j limit for a Kasner geometry: A ~ exp(iS_Regge[Kasner]). The Kasner Regge action is nonzero (unlike flat FLRW which has S_Regge = 0). The modulus |A_v[Kasner]| is the same order as |A_v[FLRW]| in the large-j limit — both are saddle points. The amplitude does not intrinsically suppress Kasner relative to FLRW in the large-j stationary-phase approximation.

**Critical assessment:** Both FLRW and Kasner are classical solutions of GR, and both are saddle points of the Regge action. The EPRL spin-foam amplitude does not suppress one relative to the other in the large-j limit. The suppression operates only for configurations that are NOT saddle points.
**Status: NOT SUPPRESSED — Bianchi I is a saddle of S_Regge just as FLRW is.**

**Class D3 — Bianchi IX mixmaster data:**
Bianchi IX also satisfies Einstein's equations and is therefore a saddle of the Regge action. The EPRL amplitude does not suppress Bianchi IX relative to FLRW in the large-j limit.
**Status: NOT SUPPRESSED.**

**Class D4 — Highly inhomogeneous data:**
This is where P08 may have traction. Highly inhomogeneous configurations with curvature varying on short scales are NOT saddle points of the Regge action (they deviate from any classical solution). Their EPRL amplitudes are suppressed by the rapidly oscillating phases at non-saddle-point configurations.

However, the suppression is relative to the nearest saddle point — which might be a highly inhomogeneous classical solution (a gravitational wave packet, a collapsing configuration, etc.). If the classical GR initial-data space contains highly inhomogeneous solutions (which it does), then those solutions are also saddle points and are not suppressed.
**Status: SUPPRESSED only for non-solution configurations — classical GR solutions of any type are saddle points and not suppressed.**

**Class D5 — Large-Weyl data:**
Same logic as D4. If the large-Weyl configuration satisfies GR's equations (e.g., a Bianchi IX solution with large anisotropy), it is a saddle of S_Regge and not suppressed. If it violates GR equations (trans-Planckian curvature, non-solution), it is suppressed.
**Status: SUPPRESSED only for trans-Planckian, non-solution configurations.**

**Class D6 — Schwarzschild data:**
Schwarzschild is an exact vacuum solution of GR and a saddle of S_Regge. Not suppressed.
**Status: NOT SUPPRESSED.**

**Class D7 — Multi-black-hole data:**
Same as D6. Not suppressed.
**Status: NOT SUPPRESSED.**

**P08 ADVERSARIAL VERDICT:**
P08 fails the adversarial test decisively for the same fundamental reason as P01. The spin-foam stationary-phase approximation suppresses configurations that are NOT solutions of GR's equations. But all of the adversarial classes D2–D7 include exact solutions of GR. Since they are solutions, they are saddle points of S_Regge, and the EPRL amplitude does not suppress them relative to FLRW.

The primitive distinguishes FLRW from non-solution configurations, but not from other classical GR solutions. R1 requires distinguishing the smooth FLRW initial state from the full space of GR solutions (including Bianchi I, Kasner, mixmaster, Schwarzschild) — and P08 cannot do this.

**Survival status after adversarial test: KILLED.** The EPRL spin-foam amplitude in the large-j (semiclassical) limit selects classical GR solutions via stationary phase. All GR solutions are saddle points of the Regge action. The primitive does not distinguish FLRW from other exact GR solutions (Bianchi I, Bianchi IX, Schwarzschild). Since the adversarial classes include exact GR solutions, P08 does not restrict Γ_GR within the classical regime.

**Exact kill reason:** Every classical GR solution is a stationary point of the Regge (spin-foam) action. The EPRL amplitude suppresses deviations from classical GR, not specific solutions within classical GR. The adversarial classes D2–D7 are exact or approximate GR solutions and are therefore not suppressed by P08.
