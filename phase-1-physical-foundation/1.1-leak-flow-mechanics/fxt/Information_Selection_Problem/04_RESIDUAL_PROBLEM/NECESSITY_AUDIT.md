# NECESSITY_AUDIT
## Gate 4 — Is New Ontology Structurally Required?

---

### Purpose

Gate 4 asks: is the residual R1 (low pre-inflationary gravitational entropy) logically incompatible with all existing ontological frameworks? Or can it be closed by a standard extension?

If existing physics can close R1 with a minor extension, no new ontology is necessary — and this program should not invent one.

---

## Section 1: Candidate Standard-Physics Closures

### Closure Attempt 1 — Fix the inflaton potential

Could a specific inflaton potential V(φ), derived from string theory or grand unified field theory, uniquely predict the pre-inflationary initial state?

**Assessment:**
- String landscape contains ~10^{500} vacua. Each gives a different V(φ) and therefore different inflation model. No unique derivation exists.
- Even if a specific V(φ) is derived, the inflaton must start in the correct part of its potential — this is itself an initial condition problem.
- **Verdict: Does not close R1.**

### Closure Attempt 2 — Establish the no-boundary proposal

If the Hartle-Hawking no-boundary proposal is shown to correctly derive low-entropy initial states, R1 is closed.

**Assessment:**
- This is the most credible candidate for closing R1 within existing frameworks (quantum gravity)
- Currently contested: Penrose's objection (no-boundary predicts high entropy) vs. Hawking-Hertog's revised analysis
- Formally incomplete: requires a non-perturbative theory of quantum gravity
- **Verdict: Live candidate. If established, R1 is closed and this program terminates.**
- **Action: Track the no-boundary debate as a live threat.**

### Closure Attempt 3 — Modify the measure on initial conditions

If a physically justified measure on GR initial data can be derived, it might show the observed initial state is actually typical (not special).

**Assessment:**
- No such measure is established. All proposed measures are either physically unjustified or make the initial state more, not less, special.
- Anthropic measures (multiverse) do not constitute a derivation — they evade the question.
- **Verdict: Does not close R1.**

### Closure Attempt 4 — Pre-big-bang or bouncing cosmology

If the universe underwent a contracting phase before the big bang (ekpyrotic, pre-big-bang scenario), smoothness could be produced by the contraction itself.

**Assessment:**
- These models do not generically produce smooth post-bounce initial conditions — they face their own fine-tuning or "mixmaster chaos" problems
- No confirmed observational signatures
- **Verdict: Shifts R1 to the pre-bounce phase; does not resolve it.**

### Closure Attempt 5 — Shape dynamics / Janus point

If shape dynamics is correct and the Janus point is a dynamical attractor, smooth initial conditions emerge from the dynamics.

**Assessment:**
- Shape dynamics is not established as the correct theory of gravity
- The Janus point attractor argument is for shape complexity, not Weyl curvature specifically
- No quantitative connection to CMB observables
- **Verdict: Alternative framework, not an established closure of R1.**

## Section 2: The Structural Argument for Necessity

None of the above closure attempts establish that R1 is resolved by existing physics. The structural argument for necessity is:

**Lemma 1:** GR as a classical theory permits all initial data satisfying the constraint equations. It does not select the low-Weyl subset.

**Lemma 2:** Inflation requires the low-Weyl initial condition; it does not derive it.

**Lemma 3:** Statistical mechanics does not apply to gravitational degrees of freedom in the early universe without a definition of gravitational entropy — and no established definition exists.

**Lemma 4:** The no-boundary proposal and the July 2026 information-saturation paper are the only existing frameworks that claim to derive low-Weyl initial conditions. Both are contested or incomplete.

**Conclusion:** If neither the no-boundary proposal nor the July 2026 paper is established, the low-Weyl initial condition is not derivable from any currently established physical framework.

This is the necessity condition: the residual R1 is not closeable by standard extensions of existing ontology, provided the no-boundary proposal remains contested.

## Section 3: What Minimum Structure Could Close R1

If R1 cannot be closed by existing frameworks, what minimum additional structure would close it?

The structure must:
1. Be definable without assuming FLRW, homogeneity, or low Weyl curvature
2. Restrict the space of physically realizable early-universe configurations
3. Select (or strongly favor) the low-Weyl subset
4. Be consistent with all established physics (GR, QFT, thermodynamics)

This structure is currently **unnamed** (per ONTOLOGY_PROHIBITION.md). It may be:
- A constraint on initial data (additional condition beyond the GR constraint equations)
- A dynamical principle (a new symmetry or conservation law)
- An information-theoretic bound (on the realizability of certain geometric configurations)
- A quantum gravity effect (beyond the no-boundary proposal)

The minimum structure is what Gate 5 (PRIMITIVE_SEARCH) will seek to characterize.
