## SECTION 3 — THE DEPENDENCY ANALYSIS

For each pair of missing objects, I determine whether one implies the other (logically depends) or whether they are independent.

### 3.1 Does F3 (mass scale / kg) imply F2 (value of g)?

**Claim:** F3 → F2. If the mass unit kg were present in $\mathcal{U}_{\text{C2}}$, it might fix $g$.

**Analysis:** If $\mathcal{U}_{\text{C2}}$ were extended to include kg, say via a new constant $\eta$ [J/info] = kg·m²/(s²·info), then $g$ could be expressed as $g = f(\eta, c, \tau, s_0, r_q)$ for some dimensional combination. The specific formula would require additional physical content (e.g., $g = \eta \cdot c^2 \tau / r_q$ is one possibility, but many others have the same units). Adding kg does not uniquely determine $g$ — it reduces $g$ to a dimensionless multiple of $\eta$-combinations, which itself requires a new equation or principle.

**Verdict:** F3 → F2 only partially. Adding kg gives the unit of $g$ but not its value. A dimensionless factor remains.

**Tighter statement:** F2 $\supseteq$ F3. The failure F2 contains F3 as a necessary sub-failure but is not identical to it. F2 = F3 + (undetermined dimensionless coefficient).

---

### 3.2 Does F4 (commutativity / O1) imply F3 (mass scale)?

**Claim:** F4 → F3 (or not).

**Analysis:** O1 (complex structure) is the extension $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$. Complex numbers provide a new algebraic structure (the imaginary unit $i$, with $i^2 = -1$) but no new dimensional unit. $\mathbb{C}$ is still a dimensionless field. Introducing complex amplitudes $\psi \in \mathbb{C}$ with $|\psi|^2 = $ probability gives the quantum state space, but:
- The Schrödinger equation $i\hbar\partial_t|\psi\rangle = H|\psi\rangle$ requires $\hbar$ [J·s] to have units of energy
- $\hbar$ requires the unit kg (since [J] = kg·m²·s⁻²)
- Therefore O1 alone does not supply kg; $\hbar$ is an additional requirement

**Verdict:** F4 does not imply F3. Complex structure can exist without an energy scale (a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space $\mathbb{C}^n$ has no preferred energy scale).

**F4 and F3 are independent failures.**

---

### 3.3 Does F5 (Ax4* / doubly stochastic) imply F4 (O1 / commutativity)?

**Claim:** F5 → F4 (or not).

**Analysis:** Doubly stochastic evolution (Ax4*) is a constraint on the transition matrices $T_m$. It requires $T_m$ to be doubly stochastic — a property of real non-negative matrices. Doubly stochastic matrices are real, and their Birkhoff-von Neumann decomposition is in terms of real permutation matrices. Nothing about doubly stochastic evolution requires complex numbers.

Conversely, quantum evolution (unitary matrices) IS doubly stochastic in a generalized sense (unitary matrices are quantum doubly stochastic, being both trace-preserving and unital when acting on density matrices). But the converse — doubly stochastic does not imply unitary — is obvious.

**Verdict:** F5 and F4 are independent. Doubly stochastic evolution is a classical probabilistic property; complex structure is algebraic. Neither implies the other.

---

### 3.4 Does F4 (O1) imply F5 (Ax4*)?

**Claim:** O1 → Ax4*.

**Analysis:** In quantum mechanics, unitary evolution $U$ is doubly stochastic in the following sense: the map $\rho \mapsto U\rho U^\dagger$ on density matrices preserves the trace ($\text{Tr}(\rho) = 1$, row conservation) and is completely positive (column conservation). Therefore quantum evolution IS Ax4*-satisfying.

More precisely: a unitary map $U$ on $\mathbb{C}^n$ induces a transition matrix $[T_U]_{j,i} = |\langle j|U|i\rangle|^2$ (the Born rule transition probabilities). This matrix is doubly stochastic: column sums = 1 (since $U$ is unitary, $\sum_j |\langle j|U|i\rangle|^2 = 1$) and row sums = 1 (since $U^\dagger$ is also unitary, $\sum_i |\langle j|U|i\rangle|^2 = \sum_i |\langle i|U^\dagger|j\rangle|^2 = 1$).

**Therefore F4 → F5 in the following sense:** If C2 had complex structure O1 (i.e., if the CCA morphism weights were complex amplitudes satisfying $|\varepsilon|^2 = $ probability and the composition was unitary), then Ax4* would hold automatically.

**F4 implies F5.** This is a key dependency.

---

### 3.5 Does F3 (mass scale / kg) imply F5 (Ax4*)?

**Claim:** kg → Ax4* (or not).

**Analysis:** Doubly stochastic evolution is a constraint on the probability transition matrices. It says nothing about energy. A classical Markov chain can be doubly stochastic (e.g., the uniform random walk on a finite graph with equal edge weights) without any energy scale. Conversely, physical systems with energy scales (e.g., a particle in a box with $E = n^2\pi^2\hbar^2/(2mL^2)$) can have doubly stochastic evolution (since quantum evolution is unitary) or not (thermal relaxation breaks Ax4* in the classical limit).

**Verdict:** F3 and F5 are independent. Mass scale and doubly stochasticity are unrelated structures.

## SECTION 4 — ATTEMPT TO UNIFY THE TWO CLUSTERS

The preliminary analysis found two independent clusters. This section exhaustively attempts to find a single hidden obstruction that generates both. Every plausible unification route is tried.

### Route U-1: Does O1 (Complex Structure) Imply the Energy Scale?

**Proposed unification:** If complex structure O1 were derived, $\hbar$ would appear as the quantum of action. $\hbar$ has units J·s, providing the energy-time scale. With $\hbar$ and the substrate parameters $c$, $\tau$, $s_0$, $r_q$, perhaps $g = g(\hbar, c, \tau, s_0, r_q)$ is fixed.

**Analysis:**

O1 (complex structure) means the state space uses $\mathbb{C}$-valued amplitudes. The complex structure is a pure algebraic extension $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C}$ — it introduces the imaginary unit $i$ satisfying $i^2 = -1$. The imaginary unit is dimensionless. $\mathbb{C}$ is a dimensionless field regardless of how it is introduced.

The Planck constant $\hbar$ enters through the Schrödinger equation $i\hbar\partial_t|\psi\rangle = H|\psi\rangle$ where $H$ has units of energy and $\partial_t$ has units s⁻¹. $\hbar$ is required to make the equation dimensionally consistent. But $\hbar$'s numerical value is:
$$\hbar = 1.055 \times 10^{-34} \text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$$

This numerical value requires an energy unit (J = kg·m²·s⁻²). Complex structure alone produces the algebraic structure of QM ($i$, Hilbert space, unitary evolution) but not the energy scale. $\hbar = 1.055\times10^{-34}$ is a dimensional constant that must be measured or derived from a theory with an energy scale.

**Formal statement:** Let $\mathcal{F}_{O1}$ be any framework with O1 (complex structure). The Schrödinger equation in $\mathcal{F}_{O1}$ reads $i\partial_t|\psi\rangle = \tilde{H}|\psi\rangle$ where $\tilde{H} = H/\hbar$ is the Hamiltonian in units of s⁻¹. The physical Hamiltonian $H$ has units of energy only after multiplying by $\hbar$. Without $\hbar$ (and hence without kg), $H$ is a dimensionless angular frequency operator.

**Conclusion:** O1 → dimensionless QM (angular frequency eigenvalues, no energy unit). O1 does not → F3 resolution (mass scale / kg). O1 and the energy scale are independent.

**Unification attempt U-1: FAILED.** Complex structure does not supply the mass scale.

### Route U-2: Does the Mass Scale (kg) Imply Complex Structure?

**Proposed unification:** If a mass scale $\eta$ [J/info] were added, it introduces an energy scale. Physical systems with energy scales exhibit quantum mechanics (via the de Broglie relation $\lambda = h/p = h/(mv)$). Does adding kg automatically produce complex amplitudes?

**Analysis:**

Classical mechanics has a mass scale ($F = ma$, energy $E = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$) but no complex amplitudes — it is a real-valued theory. Therefore mass does not automatically produce complex structure. The passage from classical to quantum mechanics is a separate step requiring the additional postulate of wave-particle duality or the canonical quantization postulate $[q, p] = i\hbar$.

**Formal countermodel:** Take Newtonian mechanics with $m$, $E = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. This has a mass scale (kg) but no complex amplitudes, no O1. Therefore kg $\not\to$ O1.

**Conclusion:** Mass scale and complex structure are logically independent. Adding kg to $\mathcal{U}_{\text{C2}}$ does not produce O1.

**Unification attempt U-2: FAILED.** Mass scale does not produce complex structure.

### Route U-3: Does Doubly Stochastic Evolution (Ax4*) Imply a Mass Scale?

**Proposed unification:** Doubly stochastic evolution preserves the uniform distribution. Perhaps this entropy-neutral evolution implies a conservation law that forces an energy scale.

**Analysis:**

Doubly stochastic evolution (Birkhoff-von Neumann: convex combination of permutations) is a property of probability transition matrices. It constrains the flow of probability, not the flow of energy. A doubly stochastic matrix has no energy content — it is a pure probability statement.

**Countermodel:** The uniform random walk on a finite graph (each vertex has equal transition probability to neighbors) is doubly stochastic but has no energy scale. The Ehrenfest model (diffusion between two urns with equal transition probability) is doubly stochastic, classical, and massless.

**Conclusion:** Doubly stochastic evolution does not imply a mass scale.

**Unification attempt U-3: FAILED.** Ax4* and kg are independent.

### Route U-4: Does Ax4* Imply O1 Independently of the F4→F5 Known Implication?

**Analysis:**

Doubly stochastic evolution exists in classical probability theory (Markov chains on a finite state space with uniform stationary distribution). Classical Markov chains satisfying detailed balance with the uniform distribution have doubly stochastic transition matrices. No complex structure is needed.

**Conclusion:** Ax4* does not imply O1. The implication is one-directional: O1 → Ax4*, not Ax4* → O1.

**Unification attempt U-4: FAILED.** Ax4* and O1 remain one-directionally related, not equivalent.

### Route U-6: Decomposing $\hbar$ — Is it One or Two Missing Primitives?

$\hbar$ has two distinct mathematical roles in quantum mechanics:

**(a) Dimensional role:** $\hbar$ introduces the unit kg (via [J] = kg·m²·s⁻²). This resolves the energy-information bridge F3.

**(b) Structural role:** $\hbar$ appears in $[q,p] = i\hbar$, making the commutator imaginary and non-zero. This introduces both complex structure ($i$) and non-commutativity. This resolves F4.

**Are these two roles separable?**

Consider a theory with:
- **$\hbar$ present dimensionally** (energy unit available) but **commutative** (classical mechanics: $[q,p] = 0$)
- **$\hbar$ present structurally** (non-commutative algebra with $[q,p] = i\hbar$) but **no mass unit** (an abstract non-commutative algebra over a dimensionless field)

The first theory (classical mechanics with energy) resolves F3 but not F4.
The second theory (abstract non-commutative algebra) resolves F4 but not F3.

**Formal proof of separability:**

**Model $\mathcal{M}_{\text{dim}}$ (F3 resolved, F4 not):** Classical Hamiltonian mechanics with $H = p^2/(2m) + V(q)$, $m > 0$, $[q,p] = 0$. This has mass (kg), energy (J), but no complex structure. F3 resolved, F4 not.

**Model $\mathcal{M}_{\text{struct}}$ (F4 resolved, F3 not):** Abstract $*$-algebra $\mathcal{A}$ over $\mathbb{C}$ with generators $\hat{q}, \hat{p}$ satisfying $[\hat{q},\hat{p}] = i\cdot\mathbf{1}$ (dimensionless commutator). This has complex structure O1 and doubly stochastic evolution (F5), but no mass unit (J is replaced by dimensionless angular frequency). F4+F5 resolved, F3 not.

**Both models satisfy all C2 axioms** (they satisfy U1–U6 and the CCA axioms). They resolve different subsets of the five failures. Therefore the two roles of $\hbar$ are mathematically separable.

**Conclusion from Route U-6:** $\hbar$ unifies the five failures but its own derivation splits into two independent components:
- A **dimensional component** (introducing kg / energy scale)
- A **structural component** (introducing non-commutativity / complex structure)
