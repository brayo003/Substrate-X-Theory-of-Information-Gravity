1. The Sanctuary (your home, but not safe)

    Appearance: A small, run‑down cabin in a clearing. Wooden walls, a creaking door, a single window that flickers with light. Inside: a table with the Book, a fireplace (can be upgraded), a storage chest.

    Function: Tension decays here. Stalker cannot enter. But if you stay too long, memory leaks in – walls start to creak, shadows move, the door rattles. Upgrades (fire pit, walls, lantern, altar) improve safety but also make the Substrate remember your hiding place faster.

    Location: Center of the map, roughly. Easy to find from any starting edge.

2. The Forest (most of the map)

    Appearance: Dense trees, undergrowth, winding dirt paths. Ambient sounds: birds, wind, occasional branch snap. At low tension, it feels peaceful. At high tension, trees seem taller, shadows deeper, sounds stop.

    Features:

        Resource nodes: Wood (from trees), stone (from rock outcroppings), food (berry bushes, mushrooms). Overharvesting depletes an area – resources respawn slower where you take too much.

        Memory scars: Areas where many players have died become “wrong” – grass grows in spirals, trees have faces, the ground pulses faintly.

        Hidden paths: The Substrate can generate new routes where you walk often. A path you use repeatedly may become a permanent shortcut.

3. Ruins (artifacts and lore)

    Appearance: Crumbled stone structures, overgrown with moss. Archways, broken pillars, half‑buried statues. Faint glow from ancient symbols.

    Function: Artifacts spawn here (Shattered Mirror, Burnt Journal, etc.). Each ruin has a unique layout – some have basements, some have hidden chambers revealed only when memory in that area is high.

    Lore fragments: Inscribed on walls, readable only when holding a specific artifact. Reveal the history of the Memoria.

4. The Origin (deepest point, endgame area)

    Appearance: A massive, buried machine – the Memoria core. Metal walls, humming cables, flickering screens displaying fragments of past players’ memories. Feels cold, technological, out of place in the forest.

    Access: Requires all artifacts and a long journey through high‑tension zones. The Stalker is more aggressive here.

    Function: Endings trigger here. You interact with the core to choose your fate.

5. Biomes (diversity across the map)
Biome	Appearance	Resources	Danger
Deep Forest	Dense, dark, little light	Wood, mushrooms	High – Stalker appears frequently
Open Meadows	Grass, flowers, sunlight	Berries, small stones	Low – but no hiding spots
Rocky Highlands	Hills, caves, cliffs	Stone, rare ore	Medium – Stalker can ambush from above
Swamp	Murky water, twisted trees, fog	Unique plants (fuel), but slow movement	High – walking in water leaves strong memory traces
Ruined City	Collapsed buildings, paved streets	Artifacts, scrap metal	Very high – Stalker knows these ruins from past players

The map is large enough that a full exploration takes 30–60 minutes, but you will never see all of it in one run – you must choose where to go.
6. Dynamic features (change with player behaviour)

    Memory trails – Your footsteps create visible, faint white lines on the ground (only you can see them). Over time, they fade, but if you walk the same path often, they become permanent – and the Stalker will use them.

    Prediction markers – When you look in a direction for more than a few seconds, a subtle red pulse appears at the spot where the Substrate expects you to go. That is where the Stalker may spawn.

    Sanctuary decay – The longer you stay inside, the more the walls darken, windows fog, and the Book’s text becomes erratic.

    Ghost echoes – After you die, your last footsteps remain as faint, blueish trails. Other players (if you later add echo mode) or your own future runs will see them.

7. Map size and grid

    Grid resolution: 10×10 studs per cell (same as your memory field). Total map size: approximately 500×500 studs (50×50 cells). This is large enough to feel expansive but small enough to remember landmarks.

    Chunks: The map is divided into 10×10 cell regions. Each region has its own baseline memory decay rate and resource spawn table.

8. Visual style

    Low poly, atmospheric – Not hyper‑realistic. Shadows, fog, and colour grading do the heavy lifting.

    Day/night cycle – Night intensity driven by global Tension. At high Tension, night falls faster and is darker.

    Screen effects – Tension adds vignette, desaturation, heartbeat sound, and slight camera sway. Shatter adds heavy distortion, inverse colours, and FOV zoom.
