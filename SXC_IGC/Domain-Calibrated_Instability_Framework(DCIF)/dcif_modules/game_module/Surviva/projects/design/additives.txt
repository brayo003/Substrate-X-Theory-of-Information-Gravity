Right now you are close to something structurally coherent:

field → prediction → stalker → tension → player behavior → field

Anything you add should feed or perturb that loop, not sit beside it as decoration.

1. Jumpscares

Verdict: allowed, but only as a function of system state—not random events.

If you implement jumpscares as:

random triggers
or time-based triggers

→ they will feel cheap and disconnected.

Instead, tie them to high local prediction certainty + proximity:

Condition structure:

if P(x) is high
AND stalker is near
AND player enters predicted cell
→ trigger jumpscare

What this does:

The scare becomes confirmation of prediction, not surprise
Player subconsciously learns: “the system knows where I will go”

That’s much stronger than a loud noise.

2. Animals (predator / prey systems)

This can work very well, but only if treated as field carriers, not NPCs.

Correct way to think about them:

Animals are:

moving disturbances in M(x), P(x), T(x)
not “gameplay content”
Example roles:
Prey (e.g., deer)
Avoid high M (player paths)
Generate low P trails
Occasionally “fake” future paths

Effect:

Introduces noise into prediction field
Makes stalker less perfectly accurate
Predators (e.g., wolves)
Follow high M gradients
Increase local T(x)

Effect:

Competes with stalker for influence
Can mislead player into unsafe regions
Risk

If animals:

attack directly
become the main threat

→ you destroy the stalker’s role as primary pressure source

So constraint:

Animals must modulate the field, not replace the antagonist.

3. Player tools

This is where your system can become genuinely deep.

Tools should modify the field equations, not just do actions.

Good tool patterns
Tool A: Memory Scrubber
Reduces M(x) locally

Effect:

Player erases their own history
Stalker loses tracking reliability

Tradeoff:

Lower M → weaker P → but less predictability
Tool B: Decoy Emitter
Artificially injects high P in a location

Effect:

Stalker is pulled to false future

This directly interacts with your new directional P(x) idea.

Tool C: Noise Generator
Adds random perturbation to P gradients

Effect:

Breaks directional coherence
Stalker movement becomes less stable
Tool D: Anchor / Sanctuary Node
Locally dampens T(x)

You already hinted this—good direction.
