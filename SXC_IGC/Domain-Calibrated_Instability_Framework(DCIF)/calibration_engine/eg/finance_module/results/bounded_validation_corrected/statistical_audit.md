## 1. Corrected 2026 OOS Result — Uncertainty

### Sample

7 trades generated by the correct `NY_Continuation` strategy (EMA crossover, hour==13 UTC) falling in the Strong_Coherent regime on 2026 EUR/USD data (Jan 1 – Jul 21, 2026).

### Point estimates

| Metric | 3-pip RT | 5-pip RT | 7-pip RT |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|
| N trades | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| PF | 0.538 | 0.326 | 0.173 |
| Win rate | 42.9% | 42.9% | 42.9% |
| Expectancy | −0.000268 | −0.000468 | −0.000668 |
| Sharpe | −0.79 | −1.38 | −1.98 |
| Max DD | 0.24% | 0.32% | 0.40% |

### Confidence intervals (3-pip RT)

**Expectancy (t-interval, 95%):** [−0.001162, +0.000625]  
Method: one-sample t-distribution, df=6. Assumes iid trades (approximate — trades are not independent; CI is wider than implied by sample size alone).

**PF (bootstrap, 95%, 5,000 replications):** [0.069, 2.691]  
Method: IID resample with replacement. CI spans 1.0 entirely. PF is a ratio of sums and highly sensitive to individual large trades.

**One-sample t-test (H0: expectancy = 0):** t=−0.73, p=0.49 — cannot reject H0.

**Effective sample size (AR(1) correction):** AR(1) coefficient = −0.621. Effective n ≈ 30. Negative autocorrelation implies slight trade independence (alternating wins/losses); the effective n is actually larger than 7 here, but the absolute information content remains very low.

### Limitations

- **7 trades cannot support any conclusion about strategy expectancy.** Confidence intervals span from large losses to positive PF by design at this sample size.
- PF is not a suitable statistic for small samples: one large winner can dominate the numerator.
- Sharpe ratio here is computed as mean/std × √n (a per-trade statistic, not annualized). It is not directly comparable to standard annualized Sharpe.
- Max drawdown underestimates true tail risk when computed from 7 trades.
- Temporal clustering: all 7 trades occurred within a 6.75-month window. Serial dependence cannot be assessed reliably.

### Power analysis

To detect a true expectancy of 0.0001 (e.g., PF slightly above 1.0) with:
- Trade-level standard deviation = 0.001
- 80% power, two-sided α = 0.05

Required sample size: **n ≈ 1,570 trades** (standard two-sample t-test formula).

This is illustrative. The required n depends critically on assumptions about trade variance, which is not yet well-estimated from 7 observations.

## 3. Random-Selection Comparison — Reassessment

### Prior method (from bounded_validation.py)

10,000 IID bootstrap replications, sampling 76 trades from a pool of 797. This did not account for temporal clustering or weekly patterns in trade opportunities.

### Corrected method A: IID bootstrap (corrected strategy)

Pool: 61 NY_Continuation signals on 2026 data, no Q filter. Sample 7 trades without replacement (7 < 61).

- n_bootstrap = 10,000
- OS PF (5-pip): 0.326
- Bootstrap mean PF: 0.343
- Bootstrap p95: 0.964
- **p-value (OS PF ≥ random):** 0.372

### Corrected method B: Block bootstrap (weekly)

Trades grouped by ISO week. Weeks sampled with replacement to build comparison samples of 7 trades. Preserves weekly clustering.

- OS PF (5-pip): 0.326
- Block mean PF: 0.346
- Block p95: 0.923
- **p-value (OS PF ≥ random):** 0.380

### Interpretation

Neither test is significant (p > 0.37). The Q filter does not improve trade selection over random picks from the same pool.

**Important caveats:**
- Both tests are severely underpowered at n=7. Non-significance cannot be interpreted as evidence that Q has no effect.
- The block bootstrap preserves weekly clustering but not intra-week order. The true dependence structure is unknown.
- The pool contains only 61 trades total; with 7 OS trades, approximately 11% of available signals fire in Strong_Coherent. The pool is small, reducing the discriminatory power of resampling.

### Null hypothesis and test statistic

- **Null hypothesis:** The OS profit factor is no greater than a randomly selected set of trades from the same pool.
- **Test statistic:** Observed PF vs empirical distribution of resampled PF.
- **Simulations:** 10,000 per method.
- **Limitation:** At n=7 trades, this test cannot rule out moderate effects. A Cohen's d equivalent of 0.3 (small-medium) would require approximately 200+ trades to detect at 80% power.

## 4. In-Sample Strong_Coherent MWU — Correction

### Verified values (reproduced in corrected_audit.py)

- SC n=41, Other n=614
- U=14,168
- p=0.089 (one-sided: H0: SC ≤ other)
- **r_biserial = −0.126**

### Interpretation corrections

The prior report noted SC PF=9.8 and described it as "looking dramatic." Three corrections are needed:

**1. Sign of r_biserial is negative.** A negative r_biserial means SC trades scored **lower** in the Mann-Whitney rank ordering than other-regime trades. The MWU tests whether a randomly selected SC trade beats a randomly selected non-SC trade on net return. SC trades do NOT achieve this on average.

**2. PF=9.8 reflects sums, not distributions.** PF is the ratio of total gross wins to total gross losses. A single very large win (e.g., +0.003 vs typical +0.0005) can create a large PF without the *distribution* of returns being better. MWU tests the distribution; PF tests the sum. These can diverge, especially with small samples.

**3. p=0.089 is not significant.** The report correctly noted this but should not imply the result is "almost significant" in a way that suggests near-equivalence. With 41 SC trades and negative r_biserial, the evidence weakly favors the null (no difference) or is ambiguous.

**Corrected statement:** In-sample (2023–2025), the 41 Strong_Coherent trades under NY_Continuation have PF=9.8, but this is not reproduced OOS, is not statistically significant (MWU p=0.089), and the per-trade return distribution does not differ significantly from other regimes (negative r_biserial).

## 5. Q Predictive Correlation — Limitations

Spearman ρ between Q(t) and forward realized vol at t+k:

| k | ρ | p | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | −0.0054 | 0.013 | 209,689 |
| 6 | −0.0074 | 0.001 | 209,689 |
| 12 | −0.0081 | 0.0002 | 209,689 |

**Statistical significance does not imply practical significance.** With n=209,689 observations, a ρ of 0.005 is detectable but explains only 0.003% of variance in future vol (ρ² × 100). This correlation has no practical utility for trading decisions.

**Autocorrelation caveat:** Both Q and future realized volatility are autocorrelated. Spearman rank correlation is less sensitive to this than Pearson, but the effective degrees of freedom are substantially smaller than 209,689. P-values should be treated as approximate. The effect would remain negative even under a conservative correction.
